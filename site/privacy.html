Privacy policy

Last updated 23 November 2026

rauntruth.com is a free research tool for viewing and analysing microscopy images, run by Ariel (ariel@rauntruth.com). It runs in your browser. The site has no server of its own: no account database and no store of anything about you. This policy says what stays on your device, and what reaches other services, and when.

Your images and results

Images are analysed on your own computer, inside your browser. They are never uploaded to us. The site sends them to no one else either, with the exceptions a person asks for: a study's owner can have it save small previews of a study folder's images into a Previews folder in that study's Google Drive root. The results stay in your browser unless you download them yourself or save an annotation, which the site saves into the study's Google Drive folders (below).

What stays on your device

To start faster, to work without the internet and to remember your choices, the site keeps these in your browser's storage on this device:

  • a copy of the site's own pages and code, so it opens without the internet;
  • Python and its packages, so the page starts without downloading them again, including those of the studies you make available offline;
  • images you opened from Google Drive (unless you turn off Auto-save images in Settings), the ones you choose in you keep when making a study available offline, small pictures of them, and Drive folders' preview files;
  • what each Google Drive folder you open in the Drive dialog holds (its files' names, sizes and dates), so the dialog opens without the internet;
  • in Chrome and Edge, for each file you open from your computer in the Image Viewer, its name and the browser's reference to it, so the recent list opens it again, never a copy of the file;
  • your settings, such as the theme, the tips you closed and your last Google Drive links;
  • your work on each image, such as your selections and corrections, and, when you press Save Draft or Save while annotating, your draft of that image: each correction in order, with the time you made it, and what the algorithm proposed, and, while the study's folder cannot be reached or your sign-in has run out, the annotation you saved, which waits here for your next click online to send it; and which images you saved an annotation of, so Load study images can mark them;
  • your last 200 actions on the page, such as the buttons you clicked, and the errors it met, never what you type or an image's name, so that Report a problem can list the last few in the GitHub issue it opens for you to read and submit;
  • when you are signed in, your name, email address and picture, and Google's sign-in token for about an hour (see below).

The avatar menu's Manage storage lists all of it, with sizes, and deletes any of it. None of it is sent to us.

Services the page reaches

  • Cloudflare serves the site's files, and counts visits with Cloudflare Web Analytics. It sets no cookies, stores nothing on your device, and doesn't follow you across sites. It receives the page's address, the page that linked to it and your browser's details, never your images or results. See Cloudflare's privacy policy.
  • jsDelivr (cdn.jsdelivr.net) delivers Python and its packages to your browser on the first visit, then again every 30 days, and, in a study that reads printed text, the engines and models that find and read it and the word list it spells what it reads by, when it first needs them; and when you make a study available offline, which also asks it how large each package is. See jsDelivr's privacy policy.
  • Google answers when you open a Google Drive link you pasted (through the Drive API, as you while you are signed in), when the page of a study opens, even for a visitor who never signs in, and reads the study's shared study file from Google Drive to learn the study's stage (no sign-in, and nothing about you is sent beyond what any request to Google carries), when making a study available offline lists, or downloads, the images in its Drive folder, when you sign in with Google, and, while you are signed in, when the page reads or saves your settings file (see below); for a study's owners and those who may save its ground truth, when the page lists, checks and adds files in that study's Drive folders (see below) and, for anyone who loads an image, lists that image's annotations in the study's Extractions folder and reads the study file again for which of them are marked, and reads back the one they open. Your profile picture and Drive's small pictures of images are loaded from Google's image servers. See Google's privacy policy.
  • YouTube (Google) is reached only when you press play on the intro video on the home page. Until then the picture shown there is one this site serves. Pressing play loads YouTube's player from youtube-nocookie.com, and Google then receives what any request to it carries, such as your browser's details and this site's address, and what you play. See Google's privacy policy.
  • GitHub is reached only when you click a link to it, for example to report a problem or propose a study. What you post there is public, under GitHub's privacy statement.

Signing in with Google

Signing in is optional; everything else works without it. When you sign in, Google asks your permission for the site to:

  • see your name, email address and profile picture, to show who is signed in in the avatar menu;
  • see, create and change its own settings file in a hidden folder in your Google Drive (the app data folder), to keep your settings the same on every device you sign in on;
  • see and download the files in your Google Drive, so that while you are signed in the Google Drive dialog opens the images and folders your account can see, as you, whether or not they are shared publicly. The site reads the folders and images you open in it and, for a study you open, its root folder's list and the study file in it, and whether you may add files to that root and to its Extractions folder, to offer you Save and, for the study's owner, Study Setup. It also asks Google, in one request each time the page loads, whether your account can read the root folder of each study the site lists, to list you the studies you may open. In Study Setup, for the study's owner, it also reads who the study's root, folders and study file are shared with (their sharing lists, with the email addresses on them), to check it against what the study's stage needs and to show who holds which access. It never changes anything with this permission.
  • see, change, create and delete only the Google Drive files it works with (Google's per-file permission), so that it can add files for you to a study's folders, as below, and change or remove only the files it added.

Google has not yet verified the site for reading your Drive, so its sign-in window warns that the app is unverified; continuing past the warning signs you in as above.

If Google Drive lets you add files to a study's Extractions folder, you may save annotations there; if it lets you add files to the study's root folder, you are the study's owner. With the per-file permission, the site adds files for you, only in the study's folders you may add files to: your annotation files, in the Extractions folder; and, for the study's owner, preview files in a Previews folder it makes in the root, markers saying an image needs ground truth, in the Extractions folder, and the study file in the root, one file which holds the study index, listing every image of the study's folders and naming the owner whose visit wrote it, the list of the study's folders, which they choose, how its images are grouped and hidden, the tags, descriptions and areas (damaged parts, the area of interest) they give the study's images and folders, and which annotation files they mark as ground truth or official; and, when they set up the study's extractions, the Extractions folder, then shared with anyone with its link to view. It changes, marks or moves to the bin only files the site itself added. It never changes, moves or deletes a file someone made outside the site, and changes who an item is shared with only as the next paragraph says.

Study Setup reads the study's sharing, and changes it only when the study's owner presses the Fix button of a correction it lists and confirms what will change: then it adds or removes the link sharing of that one folder or file of the study. Opening Study Setup, promoting a study to another stage and Check again change no sharing. Where Google does not let the site make that change, the site says so and leaves it to you in Google Drive.

The site changes nothing in your Google Drive but its own settings file, in the study folders you chose the files it adds there for you, and, on a Fix press as above, one item's link sharing. It reads the folders and images you open in its Google Drive dialog, and the study folders it shows you. The settings file holds only the settings listed in the avatar menu's Settings.

How this data is handled:

  • Accessed and used: your name, email address and picture are shown in the avatar menu. Your settings file is read when you sign in and on later visits, and replaced when you change a setting. When you save an annotation, your email address is written into your file, which is named by a hash of it. In the study folders you chose, the site reads which ones you may add files to, and adds the files you ask it to. A folder a study's owner adds by its link is checked, with the site's own key, for whether anyone with its link can open it. A folder you open in the Google Drive dialog is listed (its files' names, sizes and dates), and an image you open there is downloaded into your browser, as for a public link. In Study Setup, a study's owner sees who its root, folders and study file are shared with, read from Google when the page checks the sharing and shown on the page alone, not kept; a Fix press changes one item's link sharing, as above. Nothing else is done with any of it.
  • Stored: your name, email address, picture and Google's sign-in token (which expires after about an hour) are kept in your browser on this device until you sign out, and so is which study folders Google last said you may add files to, and which studies' root folders it last said you may read, so what you may save and your list of studies show while the token is renewed. The settings file stays in your own Google Drive, and the files the site adds stay in the study's folders. We keep no copy of any of it, anywhere.
  • Shared: your email address is in the annotation files you save, which anyone with the Extractions folder's link can read, with the time of each correction, and, for a study's owner, in the study file their visit writes, which anyone with its link can read. Otherwise Google user data goes only between your browser and Google, never to us or to any third party. It is not used for advertising, sold, or used to train any model.

rauntruth.com's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

To remove it: Sign out in the avatar menu forgets your details and the token on this device. To delete the settings file, open Google Drive's Settings, then Manage apps, find this site's app (named as on Google's sign-in window) and choose Delete hidden app data. To withdraw the site's access entirely, remove it at your Google Account's third-party connections.

Annotations

Save writes your annotation of an image into the study's Extractions folder in Google Drive, as one file per image named by a hash of your email address, holding your marks, your email address, when you made it and its history: each correction you kept, in order, with the time you made it, and what the algorithm proposed. Corrections you undid are not written. Saving again replaces your own file, and Drive keeps its earlier versions. The study's owner may mark a file as the image's ground truth or as official; the study's algorithms are scored on the files marked ground truth. Setting up the study's extractions shares the Extractions folder with anyone with its link, so anyone with that link can read every file in it, your email address inside it included, and Google Drive shows each file's owner, your name and email address, to anyone who can see it. The page itself lists, to someone who is not a member of the study, only the marked files and no one's name. Your file stays there under your Google account. To remove it, delete it in Google Drive or ask the study's owner.

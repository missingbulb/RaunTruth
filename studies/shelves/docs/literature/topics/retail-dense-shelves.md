## What specific techniques address densely packed, thin, adjacent, look-alike objects, and which are portable to a lightweight CPU pipeline?

### Takeaway
The single most transferable idea is SKU-110K's replacement of greedy NMS by a *clustering merger* over a mixture of box-Gaussians weighted by a predicted localisation-quality (IoU) score, with the number of objects set from image size over mean object size — a pure-numpy-friendly post-process that gave the biggest jump in their ablation (AP .413 -> .482, PR@.5 .491 -> .802). Close second: quadrilateral (not axis-aligned) boxes for dense tilted items (Unitail), and planogram-style neighbour-graph consistency to prune false detections (Tonioni 2017: precision ~78% -> ~98%).

### Cited Findings
- **SKU-110K problem statement**: greedy NMS "does not adequately discriminate between overlapping detections or suppress partial detections" in dense images; RetinaNet produces many overlapping, mislocalised boxes in packed scenes — [Goldman et al. 2019](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1904.00853)
- **Soft-IoU layer**: a third head predicts c^iou in [0,1] = IoU of the box with its nearest ground-truth box (0 if no overlap), trained with binary cross-entropy; total loss = classification + regression + sIoU — [Goldman et al. 2019](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1904.00853)
- **EM-Merger** (the portable part): each of N candidate boxes becomes a 2-D Gaussian at its centre with covariance diag((h/4)^2,(w/4)^2); mixture weights alpha_i = c^iou_i / sum c^iou; find K << N Gaussians minimising sum_i alpha_i min_j KL(f_i||g_j) by EM (E: assign each box to nearest cluster by KL; M: weighted mean and covariance including each box's own covariance); stop at 1e-10 or ten iterations; initialised by agglomerative hierarchical clustering (not k-means); K = image area / (mean_w * mean_h); overlapping less-confident Gaussians suppressed to K' <= K; final box per cluster = median width/height of boxes whose centres fall inside its 2-sigma ellipse — [Goldman et al. 2019](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1904.00853)
- **Ablation on SKU-110K test** (AP@.5:.95 / AP.75 / AR300 / PR@R=.5 / MAE / RMSE): Base+NMS .413/.384/.484/.491/24.96/34.38; Soft-IoU+NMS .418/.386/.483/.492/25.39/34.73; Base+EM-Merger .482/.540/.553/.802/23.98/283.97; Full .492/.556/.554/.834/14.52/23.99; RetinaNet .455/.389/.530/.544/16.58/30.70; Faster R-CNN .045 AP; YOLO9000opt .094 AP ("not suited for images with more than 50 objects") — [Goldman et al. 2019](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1904.00853)
- **Negative result**: Soft-IoU score alone with ordinary NMS barely helps (AP .418 vs .413) and slightly worsens counting — the gain comes from the merger, not the score — [Goldman et al. 2019](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1904.00853)
- **Runtime**: EM-Merger on CPU drops throughput from 0.5 to 0.23 FPS (i7-5930K + Titan X), attributed mainly to GPU/CPU memory swapping (untested claim that a GPU version would be nearly free) — [Goldman et al. 2019](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1904.00853)
- **Quadrilateral boxes**: Unitail annotates 1,777,108 products as quads on the frontal face because axis-aligned and rotated boxes overlap heavily in dense shelves, which hurts NMS; quads reduce those overlaps and handle tilted items (perspective adjusts corners); avg 145 quads/image, range 5-744; objects < 8x8 px masked rather than labelled — [Chen et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2204.00298)
- **RetailDet**: anchor-free DenseBox-style FPN detector regressing 4 corners (8 values); "quad-centerness" (distance from quad gravity centre) replaces FCOS centerness to reduce corner-regression imbalance; 57.1 g-mAP (60.3 origin / 54.1 cross-domain) vs Mask R-CNN 52.4; 6.3 FPS on a 2080Ti; segmentation-based detectors did worse than regression-based on quads — [Chen et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2204.00298)
- **Neighbour-graph consistency (classical, CPU)**: Tonioni & Di Stefano 2017 (1) match local features (BRISK etc.) to one model image per product, cluster correspondences with a Generalized Hough Transform into translation+scale pose boxes; (2) build an "observed planogram" graph — each facing a node linked to up to 8 neighbours (cardinal + diagonal), edges meaning proximity not exact alignment; when two boxes compete for the same neighbour direction keep the closer — and match it to the reference planogram as sub-graph isomorphism via heuristic branch-and-bound (coherent-neighbour fraction threshold tau = 0.25), removing inconsistent nodes; (3) for each missing facing, predict an ROI from detected neighbours and verify the expected product there by template matching/local features — [Tonioni & Di Stefano 2017](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1707.08378)
- **Stage results (70 Grocery Products shelf images, 872 instances, 181 products, IoU>0.5)**: step 1 BRISK P~78% R~75%; after graph check P~98% R~74%; after neighbour-predicted verification P~90% R~90% (F~90%); <=~15 s per image single-threaded on a laptop, ~1 s of it in the isomorphism — [Tonioni & Di Stefano 2017](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1707.08378)
- **Recurring-pattern detection (template-free)**: Liu et al. find repeated instances of the same product by pairwise visual-word/object joint assignment (2-D matrix, rows = visual words, columns = object instances; energy balances appearance similarity within rows and geometric similarity within columns), an NP-hard problem solved with GRASP; overlapping circle-representations from different regions are combined; each region capped at 25 instances; no explicit grid/periodicity analysis — [Liu et al. 2016](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1602.06647)
- **Recurring-pattern results**: overall accuracy 90.53% vs 71.84% for a template SIFT baseline; the gain is concentrated where look-alikes are hard: small products 84.44% vs 40.34%, large quantities 87.57% vs 51.69%, poor-feature products 81.24% vs 55.95%; FP <3% vs <1% for template — [Liu et al. 2016](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1602.06647)
- **Cost of repetition search**: recurring-pattern detection scales ~O(n^3) in object count (478 s vs 274 s per large-quantity case); divide-and-conquer into 3 regions cut a 39-product case from 489 s to 136 s, claimed >70% average speed gain without accuracy loss — [Liu et al. 2016](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1602.06647)
- **Class-agnostic detector + embedding**: Tonioni, Serra & Di Stefano detect "product" (one class, YOLOv2 fine-tuned on 1,247 box-only in-store images) then recognise by K-NN on a triplet-trained VGG-16 MAC embedding, with a ratio-test ambiguity threshold (tau = 0.9) and macro-category voting from confident neighbours, exploiting that same-category items sit together — [Tonioni, Serra & Di Stefano 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1810.01733)
- **U-PC** (ECCV 2018): two layers — one proposes candidate merchandise at each shelf location, a second verifies hypotheses graph-theoretically; claims >=10% better than competitors on two public datasets — [Ray et al. 2018 (ECVA)](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2018/papers_ECCV/html/Archan_Ray_U-PC_Unsupervised_Planogram_ECCV_2018_paper.php)
- **Layout as context**: the Wei et al. survey notes intra-class products tend to sit next to each other on shelves and reports a context-aware method improving 11.4% over a context-free baseline — [Wei et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7676964/)
- **Classical detection baseline**: Varol & Kuzu used a single-class Viola-Jones cascade as a generic product (cigarette-pack) detector before recognition (secondary-source description) — [search summary citing Varol & Kuzu, ICGIP 2014](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1810.01733) (method detail not verified from primary text; see Gaps)

### Inferences
- EM-Merger maps directly onto our "spacing-relative merging": candidate spines (from Sobel-line pairs, text map, colour regions) become 1-D or 2-D Gaussians along the shelf axis with width-proportional variance, weighted by a quality score; K from row length / median spine width is exactly a spacing prior. It is a few dozen lines of numpy and costs O(N*K) per iteration; for a few hundred candidates per row it is negligible on CPU. The SKU-110K ablation suggests the merger matters more than the scoring head, so even a crude score (edge strength, text-map coverage) might work. This is an inference, untested on spines.
- Their median-width box output (rather than the max-score box) is a robust choice for thin spines where boundary localisation is noisy.
- Bookshelf rows are a 1-D sequence, so Tonioni's 8-neighbour graph collapses to "left/right neighbour along a row": a cheap consistency check (e.g., a box whose width or top/bottom differs wildly from both neighbours, or overlaps both) can prune frames/posts, and Tonioni's step 3 — predict a missing item's ROI from its neighbours and verify — is a recall-recovery pattern for gaps between accepted spines (our thin crowded spines). Note it needs a reference (planogram) in retail; for books the reference would be the row's own spacing statistics.
- Unitail's quads support our leaning-item failure: a per-spine quadrilateral (two near-parallel side lines clipped by the shelf top/bottom) is closer to the truth than an axis-aligned box and reduces overlap-induced suppression. Scoring with quad-IoU is then needed.
- Recurring-pattern methods (Liu) target identical facings — analogous to a book series with identical spine design — but at O(n^3) cost and with feature-rich packaging; plain spines with little texture are their reported weak case (poor-feature 81%), so feature-matching repetition detection is unlikely to help dark plain spines.

- U-PC full method (how shelves are found, how proposals are made) could not be read: CVF HTML returned 403; PDF downloaded but not opened per constraints.
- Marder et al. ("Using image analytics to monitor retail store shelves", IBM J. R&D, 2015 per common citation) — search returned no primary source; method and numbers unverified. Tonioni 2017 reports its own recall 90.2% vs Marder's 87.4% on different datasets (not comparable) — [Tonioni & Di Stefano 2017](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1707.08378).
- Varol & Kuzu primary text not read; whether they detect shelf lines is unconfirmed (search found no evidence).

## How do they detect the shelf structure (rows, boards) and use it as a prior?

### Takeaway
Shelf structure is surprisingly rarely *detected from pixels* in the academic papers: Liu et al. take rows from the planogram XML, Tonioni builds a neighbour graph from detections; explicit shelf finding appears as Hough lines on Canny edges (Sah & Mathew 2026, patents) and as learned single-image homography for rectification (ShelfRectNet 2025). Rectification gives modest gains (~2-5 mAP points) and shrinks or reverses in dense scenes.

### Cited Findings
- Liu et al. get rows from the planogram, not the image: the parsed XML gives number of rows, products per row and their types; the shelf is split vertically into identical rows and each row horizontally into product boxes, defining per-product search regions with a margin; detections are then matched to normalised planogram positions by spectral graph matching (Leordeanu-Hebert affinity from horizontal/vertical distance terms, principal eigenvector binarised greedily) and a greedy one-to-one selection; unmatched planogram slots = empty, unmatched detections = non-compliant — [Liu et al. 2016](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1602.06647)
- Tonioni's reference planogram encodes facing counts and neighbours for a whole aisle; matching also localises the photo within the aisle — [Tonioni & Di Stefano 2017](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1707.08378)
- Hough-based shelf orientation: Canny + OpenCV 4.7.0 probabilistic Hough with constant accumulator threshold 500, tuned so shelf edges outrank product edges; dominant shelf-line angle <85 deg = LEFT, >95 deg = RIGHT in the text, but Fig. 2 gives <80 deg = Right, >110 deg = Left with 80–110 deg uncorrected (the paper contradicts itself; see [`sah-2026-hough-rectified-shelves.md`](../sah-2026-hough-rectified-shelves.md)); rectification by one of two *fixed* homographies (H_L, H_R) estimated once from representative 40-60 deg image pairs (SIFT + FLANN + RANSAC) — [Sah & Mathew 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08126v1)
- Rectification gain: YOLOv10m mAP@0.5 frontal 86.7; angled unrectified 57.1/57.8; rectified 60.8; most models +2-5 points; rectified still far below frontal — [Sah & Mathew 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08126v1)
- ShelfRectNet: ConvNeXt backbone regresses a 4-point homography from a single shelf image with the primary goal of making horizontal shelf lines parallel; synthetic-homography augmentation for small data; mean corner error 1.298 px; dataset ShelfRectSet and code "will be" released (no link at time of reading) — [Tore et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.20335)
- Patents: a USPTO retail-shelf patent detects shelves, rectifies, and maps detected shelf lines through the same homography; JP2017004505A describes Hough horizontal lines then shelf-row extraction for planogram compliance — [search summary of USPTO 9710926 and JP2017004505A](https://patents.google.com/patent/JP2017004505A/en)
- Industry practice (vendor, unverified): a second model detects whole shelves and numbers them bottom-to-top to assign products to shelves — [vendor case study via search](https://www.netsolutions.com/?p=92155)

### Inferences
- Our long-horizontal-edge shelf finder is the same family as the Hough/Canny approach; Sah & Mathew's lesson is to tune the line threshold so boards beat product (spine-top) edges, and that a single global homography is crude. A per-row rectification (rotate/shear each row so the board is horizontal and spine sides vertical) is a cheaper, more local alternative we can do in numpy; it would also normalise leaning.
- A "vanishing-point / Manhattan" rectification from near-vertical spine lines + shelf boards is plausible (arXiv 1912.01892 "Fast projective image rectification for planar objects with Manhattan structure" surfaced in search but was not read).
- Using rows as a prior: retail systems use rows to (a) bound vertical extent of every item (top/bottom = board lines) and (b) define 1-D sequences for neighbour reasoning. Both apply to spines.

### Gaps
- No academic retail paper read gives precision/recall for shelf-board detection itself.
- ShelfRectNet dataset size and speed not stated on the abstract page.

## What failure modes and negative results are reported?

### Takeaway
Recurring failures: look-alike neighbours (same-brand packaging), texture-less/small items, deformable or reflective items, distractors (banners), cross-domain drop, and steep viewpoints; and several *negative* results — Soft-IoU+NMS ~no gain, rectification hurting in dense or some angle bins, graph consistency costing recall.

### Cited Findings
- Graph consistency removes true detections lacking enough coherent neighbours (recall drops at step 2); many step-1 false positives make the isomorphism search harder; texture-less, deformable, bottles/jars unhandled; banners and textured scenes confuse proposals — [Tonioni & Di Stefano 2017](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1707.08378)
- Very similar packaging from one vendor causes false positives; small/low-texture and densely packed small items lower accuracy; poor region partitioning misses products; deformable packages unsolved — [Liu et al. 2016](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1602.06647)
- Rectification gains shrink with density: at 40+ objects/image mAP fell 18.2 -> 15.1 (right) and 19.1 -> 18.2 (left); some bins lose (110-130 deg, >10 objects: 57.1 -> 56.3); steep views can keep only ~20% of the content; depends on visible rack boundaries; authors' "best range" statements conflict (10-20 vs 5-10 objects) — [Sah & Mathew 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08126v1)
- SKU-110K: best results "remain far from perfect"; Base+EM-Merger RMSE 283.97 left unexplained (outlier counting failures); annotations checked only for obvious localisation errors — [Goldman et al. 2019](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1904.00853)
- Unitail: all detectors drop cross-domain (unseen categories, tough angles); ~2% of products have no text and many words are illegible; text only marginally helps matching on the general track (+0.56 to +0.94), text alone 31.7%/47.8% top-1 — [Chen et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2204.00298)
- Fine-grained confusion among visually similar products even when category is right; ratio-test ambiguity rejection costs recall; deformable/reflective packaging — [Tonioni, Serra & Di Stefano 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1810.01733)

### Inferences
- Our "frames/posts" false positives are the bookshelf equivalent of retail "banners/distractors": both are structures that pass a local edge/texture test but break the row's neighbour statistics — a neighbour-consistency pass is the retail answer.
- The graph-consistency recall cost argues for a two-sided design (prune by context, then *recover* by neighbour-predicted verification), not prune-only — Tonioni's final 90/90 came from adding step 3.
- Text adds little where visual signal is already strong (Unitail); for us, text is more useful as a *detection* cue on plain spines than for identification boost — inference only.

### Gaps
- No reported results specifically on very thin items (aspect ratio comparable to book spines) in retail; SKU-110K and Unitail don't break down AP by aspect ratio in what was read.

## What evaluation metrics and protocols are standard for dense shelf detection that we should adopt?

### Takeaway
Standard: COCO-style AP@[.5:.95] plus AP.75 and AR300 (SKU-110K), a precision-at-fixed-recall point, and counting MAE/RMSE per image; Unitail adds cross-domain test sets and a geometric mean (g-mAP) of in- and cross-domain mAP; classical papers use P/R/F at IoU>0.5. Splits must keep the same physical shelf out of more than one split.

### Cited Findings
- SKU-110K metrics: AP@0.50:0.05:0.95 (primary), AP.75, AR300 (recall with up to 300 detections), PR@R=.5 at IoU .75, MAE = mean |K'-t|, RMSE; 11,762 images, ~1.73M boxes, 147.4 objects/image; train 8,233 / val 588 / test 2,941; no shelf display from the same shop in two splits; images JPEG-compressed to 1 MP — [Goldman et al. 2019](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1904.00853)
- Counting transfer: full SKU-110K method MAE/RMSE 6.77/8.52 on CARPK and 7.16/12.00 on PUCPR+ — [Goldman et al. 2019](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1904.00853)
- Unitail: g-mAP = geometric mean of origin-domain and cross-domain COCO mAP (500-image cross-domain test from other stores/sensors/angles); text detection P/R/hmean; recognition normalised edit distance + word accuracy (ABINet fine-tuned: NED 0.11, 77.2%); matching top-1 on hard (2.5k) and general (10k) tracks — [Chen et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2204.00298)
- Classical protocol: precision, recall, F-measure with a detection correct at IoU>0.5 (PASCAL VOC) — [Tonioni & Di Stefano 2017](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1707.08378)
- Planogram counting metric: per product type 1 - |N_matched - N_expected| / N_expected, averaged — [Liu et al. 2016](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1602.06647)
- Retail datasets (Wei et al. survey Table 5): GroZi-120 (120 classes; 676 train / 4,973 test frames, on-shelf); GroZi-3.2k (8,350 train / 680 test, 27/80 classes); Freiburg Grocery (4,947 / 74, 25 classes); Cigarette (3,600 / 354 shelf images, 10 classes, boxes — the Varol & Kuzu set per other citations); Grocery Store (81 classes); D2S (60 classes, pixel instance, checkout); RPC (53,739 train / 30,000 checkout, 200 classes, 17 metaclasses). Survey has some table/text inconsistencies. Survey is CC BY 4.0 — [Wei et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7676964/)
- Grocery Products (George & Floerkemeier): 8,350 product images in 80 categories, 680 shelf images; Tonioni added instance boxes for 70 of them — [Tonioni & Di Stefano 2017](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1707.08378)
- Wei et al. survey challenges: large-scale classification, data limitation, intra-class variation (differences only in flavour text/size), flexibility (new products, catastrophic forgetting) — [Wei et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7676964/)

### Inferences
- With 133 labelled books, report P/R/F at IoU 0.5 (current practice, comparable to classical retail) *and* AP@[.5:.95]/AP.75 so tight-localisation of thin spines is visible (an IoU-0.5 hit on a 15-px-wide spine can be off by a whole neighbour); add per-image count MAE, which directly measures merge/split errors. For leaning books, consider quad- or rotated-IoU.
- Adopt the SKU-110K split rule: never let photos of the same physical shelf appear in both tuning and held-out sets.
- A Unitail-style cross-domain held-out set (different homes/phones) is the analogue of their g-mAP.

- WebMarket and Holoselecta details (size, licence) not retrieved — Holoselecta and WebMarket are absent from the Wei survey and searches returned nothing primary.
- SKU-110K dataset licence (GitHub) not verified; Unitail licence not stated on the paper page.
- Archive/warehouse box inventory and binder/file-folder spine recognition: no dedicated paper or dataset found; nearest are library book-spine systems (e.g., a 2016 arXiv scene-text approach to spine recognition, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1611.07385v1.pdf; a 2024 SPIE call-number OCR misplacement system) which belong to the book-spine literature rather than retail — [search results](https://medicalimaging.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/13396/133960A/Design-of-a-book-misplacement-detection-system-based-on-image/10.1117/12.3050429.full).

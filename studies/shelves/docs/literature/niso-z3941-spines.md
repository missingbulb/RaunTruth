# ANSI/NISO Z39.41-1997 — Printed Information on Spines

*National Information Standards Organization, "Printed Information on Spines", ANSI/NISO Z39.41-1997 (revision of
Z39.41-1990; first issued 1979 as *Book Spine Formats*), approved by ANSI 14 January 1997 · NISO Press, Bethesda,
1998 · ISBN 1-880124-32-7, ISSN 1041-5653 · niso.org · © 1998 NISO, all rights reserved (no reproduction without
permission; these notes paraphrase and quote clauses briefly) · PDF: study Drive folder
Literature/niso-1997-z3941-printed-info-spines.pdf.*

**Why it matters here:** it is the norm for *where* on a spine the title, the author and the publisher go and
*which way* the title runs, including for Hebrew. It gives our reader priors on layout and turn, and our matcher a
list of what a spine may legitimately leave out.

---

## 1. Scope and purpose (§1)

- **§1.1** The aim is a standard place for a *library's* local data (call number, labels) so it does not hide the
  publisher's spine text, and every spine item stays visible.
- **§1.2** Applies to spines of printed bindings, covers, containers and other enclosures: books, software,
  video, microform, music, combinations; commercial and library-bound alike. Works with no spine
  (saddle-stitched) are excluded.
- **§3 "Spine"**: an enclosure whose spine is *too narrow to carry information* counts as having none and is out of
  scope; no width is given.

## 2. What goes on a spine (§4)

- **§4.1 Bibliographic information:** the title; numbering (alphabetical, numerical, chronological); edition.
- **§4.1.1 Responsibility:** name(s) of the principal entities (authors, editors, composers, performers,
  compilers) "should be provided"; **normally only one type** of entity is given.
- **§4.1.2 Title:** "as full a version as possible" of the chosen title, plus a subtitle if needed to identify it.
  In multipart works each part may carry, besides the collective (series) title, its own monographic title. The
  spine's title should match the **title page** (for a book).
- **§4.1.2.1 Title in more than one language:** the title the publisher intends the work to be known by is
  **placed first, or is the only one** on the spine.
- **§4.1.3** Series, volume or part number or name, and year/season/month, when needed to tell the item apart.
- **§4.1.4** Edition statement, when needed, in the form the work itself uses.
- **§4.2 Library information:** call number, logo, other library data — handwritten, a label, a barcode, or
  embossed.
- **§4.3 Publisher information:** name, logo, catalogue number. **The ISBN does not appear on the spine**: Z39.21
  puts it at the foot of the outside back cover.
- **§3 definitions of title forms:** analytic, collective (series), monographic, parallel (the other-language
  title), subordinate (subtitle).

## 3. Arrangement and space (§5)

- **§5** Spine information "shall" be arranged in **three areas** (Fig. 4), top to bottom:
  1. **Bibliographic identification area** — the uppermost part: author, title, numbering, edition (§3, §4.1).
  2. **Library identification area** — **1.5 in (40 mm)**, immediately below it (§3, §5.1 "shall").
  3. **Publisher identification area** — the **bottom 1.5 in (40 mm) or more** (§3, §5.1 "shall").
- **Order and format within each area are left to the publisher, producer or library** (§5).
- **§5.1** Space permitting, bibliographic data may start **0.5 in from the top** to leave room for library
  designations such as "Reference", "Reserve", "Oversize".

*These are a standard's prescriptions, not measurements: how far publishers follow them, in the US or elsewhere,
the document does not say.*

## 4. Typography and title direction (§6)

- **§6.1** Items of §4.1.1–4.1.3 may be set **descending, pillar or transverse**, or a mix, as long as the library
  area stays free; if descending or pillar leaves too little room, a mix "should" be used.
- **§3 / §6.2 / Fig. 5, four orientations:**
  - **Descending-spine title** — along the spine's length, readable when the work **lies flat**. *For Roman
    scripts, left to right with the work lying flat, front cover uppermost. "For non-Roman scripts that are printed
    right to left (e.g., Hebrew) the direction of the title should be reversed, i.e., right to left."*
  - **Pillar-spine title** — one character per line, read top to bottom with the work upright.
  - **Transverse-spine title** — characters side by side across the spine, readable with the work upright.
  - **Edge title** (for works with no spine; included "for clarity") — along the left edge of the front cover for
    Roman scripts; **for right-to-left scripts such as Hebrew, along the right edge, read right to left**.
- **§6.2** "The position of the characters in a title relative to the spine will vary with the language of the
  title." The **ascending** spine (bottom to top, common in continental Europe) is **not** among the four styles.
- **§6.3** Any area's data may be **abbreviated, replaced by graphic symbols, or sized to fit** at the producer's
  discretion.

**The Hebrew clause, worked through (our inference, not the standard's words):** a Latin book lying flat, front
cover up, spine toward the reader, has its head on the left, so left-to-right text runs head to foot; standing on
the shelf, its letters' tops face the front cover, on the spine's right, so the title is turned a quarter
**clockwise** and reads top to bottom. A Hebrew book's front cover is the other one: lying flat front up, spine
toward the reader, its head is on the right, so right-to-left text again runs **head to foot**; standing, its
letters' tops face the front cover, now on the spine's *left*, so the title is turned a quarter
**counter-clockwise**. If that reading holds, a standard Hebrew spine and a standard English spine both read
downward but need **opposite quarter turns** to set upright. The standard's one sentence does not spell this out,
and it says nothing of how Israeli publishers actually print.

## 5. Gaps — what the text does not state

- **Hebrew and right-to-left are *not* absent** (contrary to the expectation given these notes): §6.2 names Hebrew
  for the descending and edge titles. What is absent: Hebrew for pillar or transverse titles; which cover is a
  right-to-left book's front cover (it is implied, not defined; §3 "Cover" defines the front cover only by reading
  order, "the contents in the intended sequence for use"); mixed-script spines (a Hebrew title with a Latin
  publisher logo); bidirectional text.
- The ascending style; any reading-direction rule for Roman scripts other than descending.
- The order of author and title inside the bibliographic area, and any separator (our shelves' `author / title`
  slash comes from publishers, not from this standard).
- The minimum spine width that counts as a spine; typefaces, sizes, colours, contrast.
- Whether the series title or the volume title should dominate when both appear.
- How widely it is followed, and whether it was reaffirmed or withdrawn after 1997 (the foreword says it would
  next be reviewed in 2001).
- Any non-US practice; it is an American national standard.

## 6. What to take / where we diverge (our inference)

- **A turn prior by script.** Our reader tries both quarter turns of a tall crop and keeps the most confident
  (WARMUP, Read). If the worked reading of §6.2 holds, a Hebrew spine wants the counter-clockwise turn and an
  English one the clockwise turn. That is checkable: the ground truth keeps each book's turn and its language, so
  the share of Hebrew books at each turn can be counted before anything is built. A prior should only break near
  ties, since publishers deviate.
- **Layout priors for a matcher.** Title and author near the top, publisher in the bottom 40 mm or more, a library
  label in between on library books. A reading split into lines can weight the upper text as title/author and the
  foot as publisher, as HEBREW.md's "spine as a language" already observes on our shelves.
- **What the matcher must tolerate, by the standard's own leave:** one author of several (§4.1.1), a series title
  beside the volume title (§4.1.2), abbreviations and logos in place of words (§6.3), a parallel title omitted
  (§4.1.2.1). **No ISBN on the spine** (§4.3): a matcher cannot expect an identifier from a spine photo.
- **Transverse and pillar titles** read across a tall crop or one letter per line; our readers' turn search does
  not cover a pillar title, which no reader of whole lines will read. A gap, not yet seen in our photos.
- **Divergence:** our shelves are home shelves of Hebrew trade books, without library labels; the three areas are a
  prior, not a rule our finder may depend on.

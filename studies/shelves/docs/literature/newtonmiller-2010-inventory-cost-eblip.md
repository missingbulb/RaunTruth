## Why this matters to our task

Indirect. It gives the library-side baseline any shelf-reading tool competes with, and the shape of misplacement:

- **Speed to beat:** barcode scanning with a laptop and hand scanner ran at a **mean 8.35 s per book** (mode 5 s),
  707 h for 305,000 books. A photo of a shelf of ~20 books would replace about 3 minutes of scanning (our
  inference, not the paper's).
- **Misplacement is mostly local:** 82% of misshelved books were within 25 books of their place. A shelf-order checker
  working within one photo would see most of it; the rest are far away and need a catalogue.
- **"Missing" is often a records problem:** 30% of the 228 books catalogued "Missing" were in the right place.
- **Labels and catalogue disagree:** 565 call-number labels differed from the catalogue — a reader of spine labels
  inherits that error rate.

It says nothing about images, reading spines, home libraries, or accuracy of the scan itself (scanning errors were
logged but not reported).

## Method (Sung et al., as summarised)

- **Design:** bibliometric study and cost-benefit analysis.
- **Setting:** medium-sized academic library, rural US. Scanned: ~300,000 books in LC classes D–H, N, P and Q, two
  thirds of the monograph collection (the costing scales to a "half-million monograph collection").
- **System:** in-house "Library Stacks Management System": a laptop with a hand-held barcode scanner, an MS Access
  database holding a shelf-list and an active-status list. It alerted the worker when a book had an active status
  (Missing, Renewed, Overdue, Charged), was out of order, or was not in the system. Each scan logged a timestamp (to
  the second), call number and barcode. After each section it listed each misshelved book's distance from its place
  and the time between scans. Analysis in SPSS 15.0.
- **Distance unit:** books. Per the reviewer, a footnote converts a shelf: 27 inches of books at an assumed 1 inch per
  book ≈ 25 books. "Badly" misshelved = more than 25 books away, i.e. off its own shelf.
- **Cost model:** $440,000 labour (selection, acquisition, cataloguing salaries) to buy 15,000 monographs in a year
  ≈ **$30 labour per book**; interlibrary loan ≈ $30 per transaction (from a study over ten years old); patron time
  30 minutes per misplaced book; scanning at 8 s per book and $10/hour.
- Inventory completed end of 2006; reuse counted to July 2008.

All from the summary's *Main Results*; none from a table.

| Time between scans | mode 5 s (80% of barcodes); mean 8.35 s |

| Found on shelf with active status | 291: Charged 4, Overdue 7, Renewed 4, In Transit 24, Missing 228 |

| Reuse by July 2008 | 52% of the 291 active-status; > 36% of those > 25 books away; 17% of all scanned |
| Label vs catalogue call number mismatches | 565: 40% → ≤ 10 books away; 10% → 10–100; 35% → > 100 |

Consistency checks (ours): 305,000 × 8.35 s ≈ 707 h, and 8 s at $10/h ≈ 2.2 ¢, both agree. The "Missing" shares sum
to 90% and the label shares to 85%; the remainder is not reported. 5,300 badly misshelved books are ~1.7% of those
scanned; the total misshelved count, and so the overall misshelving rate, is **not given**.

## Reviewer's critique worth keeping

- Distance in books rests on the 1-inch-per-book assumption buried in footnotes; Anderson (1998) measured major
  errors in shelves before/after the correct one, which staff count more naturally.
- Workflow signals left unexamined: the 24 Miscellaneous-status books on open shelves, and the 35% of label errors
  that sent books > 100 away.
- The 30% of "Missing" books in place suggests checking the shelf before changing a catalogue status.
- The cost-benefit is hard to replicate: salaries only in footnotes, no job descriptions, no development, maintenance
  or training cost of the in-house system, old ILL costs, no comparison with vendor purchasing plans, and no
  comparison against traditional shelf reading.

## Gaps — what the summary never states

- Total misshelved books and the overall misshelving rate; the count found within 25 books.
- Scanning error rate (logged, not reported).
- The library's name; staff count; how the 516 unfound were searched.
- The original's tables and figures (not seen).

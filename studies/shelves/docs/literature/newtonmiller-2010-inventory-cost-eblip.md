## What it gives the study

Context only: the library baseline a shelf-photo tool would compete with, and the shape of misplacement. Nothing
here bears on boxing or reading spines.

- **The speed to beat:** a laptop and hand-held barcode scanner ran at a **mean 8.35 s per book** (mode 5 s, 80% of
  barcodes), ~707 h for 305,000 books. A photo of a ~20-book shelf would stand in for about 3 minutes of scanning
  (our inference). Our Find books takes 27 s on the 60-book `1zktX1` (WARMUP), well inside that.
- **Misplacement is mostly local:** 82% of misshelved books were within 25 books of their place, so a check inside
  one photo would see most of it. "Badly" misshelved means more than 25 books away, from a footnote converting a
  27-inch shelf at an assumed 1 inch per book; 5,300 books were that far.
- **"Missing" is often a records problem:** 30% of the 228 books catalogued "Missing" were in the right place.
- **Labels and catalogue disagree:** 565 spine call-number labels differed from the catalogue, so a reader of spine
  labels inherits an error the photo cannot fix.

What it does not give: images, spine reading, home libraries, or the scan's own error rate (logged, not reported).
The total misshelved count, and so the overall misshelving rate, is not given; 5,300 badly misshelved books are ~1.7%
of those scanned (our arithmetic). All numbers are second-hand from the reviewer; the original was not read, and the
reviewer notes the book-distance unit rests on the 1-inch-per-book assumption.

Left out: the cost model (≈ $30 labour per replacement book, 2.2 ¢ per book scanned), the reuse figures, the status
breakdowns and the reviewer's critique of the cost-benefit method; cross-checked against all four pages.
